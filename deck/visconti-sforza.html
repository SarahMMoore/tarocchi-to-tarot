Visconti-Sforza

The Visconti-Sforza Tarot is a collection of luxury, hand-painted decks created in Milan, Italy, during the mid-15th century. Attributed to artist Bonifacio Bembo and his workshop, these exquisite cards were commissioned by the ruling Visconti and Sforza dynasties as courtly art pieces and tactical card games rather than tools for modern divination.

Key Historical Facts

  • The 15th-Century Mastery: Painted predominantly around 1450, these decks celebrated the marriage and dynastic union of Bianca Maria Visconti and Francesco Sforza. Bonifacio Bembo crafted them using tempera, precious gold leaf, and silver accents layered on thick pasteboard, making them some of the most luxurious artifacts of early Renaissance art.
  • The Pierpont Morgan-Bergamo Survival: As the most famous and complete variant of the decks, 74 of its original 78 cards have survived the centuries. The deck is missing only four cards—the Devil, the Tower, the Three of Swords, and the Knight of Coins—making it an invaluable blueprint for the classic 78-card Tarot structure.
  • A Fragmented Modern Legacy: The surviving physical masterpieces no longer rest in Milan. Instead, they are split across three prestigious global locations: the Morgan Library & Museum in New York (35 cards), the Accademia Carrara in Bergamo (26 cards), and the Colleoni family's private collection (13 cards).
