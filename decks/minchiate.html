Minchiate di Firenze 1860-1890 (Tarocchi fiorentini)

The Minchiate di Firenze is an expanded, 97-card variant of Tarot that originated in Florence, Italy, during the early 16th century. Below are the major cards organized in an optimized 5-column grid mapping the Renaissance cosmos.

HISTORICAL NOTE:

  • The Structural Expansion: To deepen the gameplay and trick-taking mechanics, Florentine creators added 20 new trumps to the traditional lineup. These additions included the 12 signs of the zodiac, the four classical elements (Fire, Water, Earth, Air), and the three theological virtues (Faith, Hope, Charity) along with the cardinal virtue of Prudence.
  • The "Papal" Alterations: Minchiate famously eliminated the Popess (High Priestess) card. It also modified the imagery of the remaining traditional "Papi" (the Pope, the Empress, the Emperor, and the Hierophant), replacing them with secularized or westernized representations of Eastern rulers (the Grand Duke, the Western Emperor, the Eastern Emperor, and the Lover) to avoid religious censorship during card play.
  • The 1860–1890 Era: This era marks the twilight of the deck’s widespread publication in Italy. During the late 19th century, printers in Florence and Bologna produced the deck using refined woodblock printing and hand stenciling, right before industrialization and the rise of the standard 52-card deck permanently eclipsed regional Tarocchi variations.
